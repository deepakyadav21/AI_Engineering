Evaluating Delivery Strategies: A Simulation Study for We-Doo
Module Name: Modelling, Simulation and Optimisation

This research presents a simulation study evaluating daily parcel delivery strategies for the 'We-Doo' delivery service company. The customer location data used in this study was generated based on the last four digits of the student identification number, resulting in a map with 100 customer locations. The simulation covers a period of 50 days of delivery to analyze the effectiveness of the proposed strategies. The study employed various algorithms, including the Floyd-Warshall algorithm, to optimize delivery routes and maximize delivery process efficiency. Using a discrete-event simulation approach, the simulation models evening parcel delivery would run in a selected commuter township. Key factors considered in this simulation include parcel distribution, route planning, driving times, customer response times, and the capacity of the electric cargo bike.

Addressing Routing Challenges Through Genetic Algorithms
Module Name: Modelling, Simulation and Optimisation

This research deeply investigates the progression of applying genetic algorithms (GA) to optimize routing challenges. It explores the conclusions drawn from four influential papers originating from different time periods. Over the course of the past forty years, GA has risen as a significant technique for managing complex routing issues. This investigation provides a historical perspective on the evolution of GA-driven optimization for media routing. This is achieved by analyzing four noteworthy research works spanning various decades. The 1980s saw the inception of the traveling salesman problem, a pioneering study that laid the groundwork for the role of genetic algorithms in optimizing routes. The 1990s witnessed a shift towards prioritizing time-sensitive routes. The 2000s introduced the concept of multi-objective optimization to address routing problems. Progressing to the 2010s, hybrid solutions emerged, breaking through traditional limitations and effectively tackling intricate routing challenges. Additionally, the enduring impact of GA on enhancing routing optimization is demonstrated. The thorough analysis of these historic studies further emphasizes their vital role in pushing the boundaries of efficiency, sustainability, and innovation in the contemporary fields of logistics and transportation.

Sentiment Analysis in Drug Reviews using Machine Learning and Deep Learning Techniques
Module Name: Domain Application of Predictive Analytics

The rapid growth of social media and sites for customer feedback has resulted in a plethora of user-generated reviews, particularly medicine reviews. These reviews are useful to healthcare practitioners, pharmaceutical businesses, and regulatory authorities because they provide thorough information about the effectiveness and safety of a medicine.

Hypothesis: Through the application of advanced natural language processing (NLP) techniques, our goal is to build a robust model capable of properly predicting sentiment polarity in medication reviews. This model is expected to provide valuable insights on user perceptions regarding medication outcomes. We contend that the strength of feelings is closely linked to a variety of factors, including the frequency of reported side effects and the severity of the medical condition under treatment. Our hypothesis proposes a high correlation between the sentiment expressed in user-generated reviews about medical treatments and drugs, and the perceived efficacy and safety of these products. Positive sentiment is thought to be connected with feedback of efficacy and minimal side effects, whereas negative sentiment is supposed to be associated with expressions of ineffectiveness and negative side effects.

Tools Used: Natural Language Processing (NLP) techniques, Machine Learning algorithms, Deep Learning models, Python programming language, TensorFlow, scikit-learn, Keras.

Butterfly and Moth Species Detection and Classification Using Deep Learning
Module Name: Research Project

Butterflies bring to mind images of meadows, colorful flower filled landscapes and lively summer gardens. Aside from their appeal, areas rich in butterflies and moths indicate an ecosystem that supports a wide variety of invertebrate populations. This research delves into the classification of images specifically focusing on identifying 100 species of butterflies and moths. The dataset used consists of 12,594 training images 500 validation images and 500 test images all sized at 224 x 224 pixels. By employing four models including pretrained models such as EfficientNetB0, ResNet50, VGG19 Models and a custom CNN model named ButterflyNet. ResNet50 stands out with a test accuracy of 95% closely followed by EfficientNetB0 at 93.60% VGG19 Model at 92.80% and ButterflyNet at 85.40%. Moreover incorporating an interactive Streamlit UI enhances accessibility by allowing users to conduct real time tests. In conclusion ResNet50 emerges as the model while ButterflyNet shows promising potential. Future efforts should explore tuning techniques, ensemble methods and continuous model optimization to contribute to the evolving field of image classification and its crucial role, in biodiversity conservation through technological advancements.
